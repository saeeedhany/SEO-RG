What is SEO

The discipline of being found

Search Engine Optimization is the practice of making content visible, credible, and relevant to search engines — so they surface it to people who are actively looking for it. Every day, over 8.5 billion searches are made on Google alone. Most of those clicks go to the first three results. SEO is how you get there.

It is not about gaming algorithms. Google has spent two decades closing every shortcut, and the 2024–2025 era of Helpful Content and AI Overviews has made genuine quality the only durable advantage. The fastest path to ranking is also the honest one: create content that genuinely answers what people are searching for, on a site that is technically sound and trusted by others.

"SEO is not a trick. It is the discipline of making your content as discoverable and as trustworthy as it deserves to be."
Organic Traffic

Earned, not rented

Paid ads stop the moment the budget stops. A page that earns a top-3 ranking can generate traffic for years at zero cost-per-click.

Compounding Returns

The long game

SEO compounds. A strong content library built in 2022 still outranks 2025 competitors who built nothing. The work done today keeps paying.

53% of all web traffic

The dominant channel

Organic search drives more traffic than social media, email, and paid search combined. No other channel has that reach at that cost.

How SEO is structured

Four disciplines. One field.

SEO is not a single skill. It is a field composed of four distinct disciplines, each with its own specialists, tools, and career paths. Every SEO problem belongs to one of them — and most real-world problems involve more than one.

The Learning Path

From zero to specialist

SEO has a natural learning sequence. Skipping layers leads to wasted effort — building great content on a broken technical foundation, or pursuing links before the on-page work is done. Follow this order.

Phase 0 — Foundation Understand the field before you work in it
Step 01

Understand what SEO is and why it matters

The channel, the compounding returns, the scale. You are doing this step right now.

This page
Step 02

Learn how search engines actually work

Crawl, render, index, rank — and what SERP features mean for clicks. The mental model that makes every other SEO decision make sense.

→ How Google Works — SEO Field Overview
Step 03

Study the complete ranking factor landscape

Know which signals matter most, which are myths, and how they've shifted in 2024–2025 with Helpful Content and AI Overviews.

→ Ranking Factors — SEO Field Overview
Phase 1 — Technical Foundation Content can't rank if the site can't be crawled
Step 04

Run your first technical audit

Use Screaming Frog or Ahrefs to crawl your site. Fix broken links, redirect chains, duplicate titles, missing meta, and mobile issues. Do this before any content work.

→ Technical SEO Handbook
Step 05

Fix Core Web Vitals

LCP, INP, and CLS are Google's page experience signals. Measure them in PageSpeed Insights and Search Console — then fix the causes, not the symptoms.

→ Ch.04: Core Web Vitals
Step 06

Implement structured data and sitemaps

Schema markup enables rich results in the SERP — review stars, FAQs, breadcrumbs. XML sitemaps accelerate crawl discovery. Both are high-leverage, low-effort wins.

→ Ch.06: Structured Data
Phase 2 — Content & On-Page Tell Google — and people — exactly what each page is about
Step 07

Build a keyword and topic strategy

Map keywords to intent. Group by head terms, mid-tail, long-tail. Assign each URL a single target keyword. Identify content gaps against the pages that currently rank.

→ Content Writer's Handbook
Step 08

Write optimised titles, meta descriptions, and headings

Title tags are your SERP ad copy. H1–H3 hierarchy signals page structure. These elements have the highest impact-to-effort ratio of any on-page factor.

→ Ch.03: Title Tags & Meta
Step 09

Match content to search intent

Informational, navigational, commercial, transactional — each query type needs a different content format and depth. Matching intent beats keyword density every time.

→ Ch.02: Search Intent
Phase 3 — Authority & Links Build the external trust signals that Google uses to rank competing pages
Step 10

Create link-worthy assets

Original research, comprehensive guides, free tools, and proprietary data earn links naturally. These compound over time and reduce dependence on outreach.

→ Link Building Handbook
Step 11

Run targeted outreach and digital PR

One link from a DR-80 publication outweighs fifty low-quality links. Target relevance and authority over volume. Guest posts, expert quotes, and collaborative research all work.

→ Ch.04: Digital PR
Step 12

Audit and disavow toxic links

Spammy backlinks can actively suppress rankings. A regular link audit in Ahrefs or Search Console, combined with strategic disavows, protects your authority baseline.

→ Ch.10: Toxic Link Auditing
Phase 4 — Local & E-E-A-T For businesses with physical presence, and every site that needs Google to trust it
Step 13

Optimise your Google Business Profile

GBP is the highest-leverage local SEO action available. Complete every field, add services and photos, post updates regularly, and respond to every review — positive and negative.

→ Local SEO Handbook
Step 14

Build NAP consistency and local citations

Name, Address, Phone must be identical across every directory, website, and social profile. Even minor inconsistencies confuse Google's entity resolution and suppress local pack rankings.

→ Ch.04: NAP & Citations
Step 15

Build E-E-A-T signals across the site

Author bios, credentials, external citations, expert quotes, and brand mentions across trusted publications all signal experience and trustworthiness to Google's quality systems.

→ Ch.11: E-E-A-T Strategy
Phase 5 — Measure & Iterate SEO without measurement is guessing. Make decisions from data.
Step 16

Set up your measurement stack

Google Search Console (free, non-negotiable), GA4 (traffic), Ahrefs or Semrush (rankings and backlinks). These three tools cover the vast majority of what you need to track.

→ Quick Answers: Tools Reference
Step 17

Track rankings and diagnose movement

When rankings drop, the cause is almost always one of three things: a core algorithm update, a technical regression, or a stronger competitor. Each requires a different response.

→ Quick Answers: Algorithm Updates
Step 18

Run content refreshes on decaying pages

Content ages. Pages stuck in positions 4–10 often need a refresh — updated data, deeper coverage, stronger internal links — to move into the top 3. This is faster than creating new content.

→ Ch.11: Publishing & Refresh Checklist
