A

Algorithm #
Google's algorithm is the set of rules and machine learning models that determine how pages are ranked in search results. It encompasses hundreds of signals and is updated thousands of times per year — most updates are minor, but several named "core updates" per year can cause significant ranking shifts.
Anchor Text #
The clickable, visible text of a hyperlink. Anchor text is a relevance signal — it tells Google what the linked page is about. A healthy backlink profile contains varied anchor text: branded, naked URL, generic ("click here"), and some keyword-rich. Profiles dominated by exact-match keyword anchors risk Penguin-related suppression.
Authority #
A measure of a domain or page's trustworthiness and influence in the eyes of search engines, primarily derived from the quality and quantity of its backlink profile. Third-party tools represent this as Domain Rating (Ahrefs), Domain Authority (Moz), or Authority Score (Semrush). Google's own internal metric is PageRank, which is no longer publicly displayed.
AI Overviews #
Google's AI-generated summaries that appear above organic results for many informational queries. Introduced broadly in 2024, they answer questions directly in the SERP, reducing organic clicks for covered queries. Pages cited within AI Overviews receive brand exposure and indirect traffic even without direct clicks.

B

Backlink #
A hyperlink from one website to another. Backlinks are the primary off-page ranking signal — Google interprets them as votes of confidence from one site for another. The quality of the linking domain, the relevance of the linking page, the placement within content, and the anchor text all affect how much ranking value (PageRank) a backlink passes.
Bounce Rate #
In GA4, a bounce is a session where no engagement event occurred within 10 seconds. High bounce rates on content pages are not inherently negative — a user who reads an article and leaves satisfied is a successful visit. However, high bounce rates combined with low time-on-page on transactional pages can signal a mismatch between content and search intent.
BERT #
Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers — a Google neural network update (2019) that improved Google's ability to understand the context and nuance of words in a query. Rather than treating each word independently, BERT interprets words in relation to all other words in the sentence. Effective for conversational and long-tail queries.

C

Canonical Tag #
A <link rel="canonical" href="..."> element in the <head> that tells Google which URL is the preferred version of a page when multiple URLs serve similar or identical content. A signal, not a directive — Google may choose to ignore a canonical if it disagrees with the page's content.
Click-Through Rate (CTR) #
The percentage of SERP impressions that result in a click. CTR varies significantly by position (position #1 averages ~27.6% CTR on desktop), query intent, and SERP feature presence. A well-optimised title tag and meta description can lift CTR by 20–30% without any change in rankings.
Core Web Vitals (CWV) #
Google's page experience metrics, used as a ranking signal since 2021. The three metrics are: LCP (Largest Contentful Paint — loading performance, threshold <2.5s), INP (Interaction to Next Paint — responsiveness, threshold <200ms, replaced FID in 2024), and CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift — visual stability, threshold <0.1). Measured from real field data in Search Console.
Crawl Budget #
The number of URLs Googlebot will crawl on a site within a given timeframe, governed by crawl rate limit (server capacity) and crawl demand (page popularity and freshness). Most small sites do not need to worry about crawl budget — it becomes critical for large sites (100K+ URLs) where important pages may not be crawled frequently enough.

D

Disavow #
A Google Search Console tool that allows site owners to tell Google to ignore specific backlinks when assessing their site's link profile. Used when a site has accumulated toxic or manipulative links that may be causing ranking suppression. Should be used cautiously — incorrectly disavowing good links can harm rankings.
Domain Rating (DR) #
Ahrefs' proprietary metric (0–100) representing the strength of a website's backlink profile. Higher DR generally correlates with stronger organic rankings, but it is a third-party metric — Google does not use DR directly. Useful for comparing relative link authority between domains.

E

E-E-A-T #
Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness — the framework Google's quality raters use to assess page quality, particularly for YMYL content. Not a direct algorithmic ranking signal but the qualitative standard that Google's systems are trained to approximate. Building E-E-A-T means: credentialed authors, first-hand experience demonstrated in content, citations from external authoritative sources, and brand presence across trusted publications.
