How to Use This Reference

When rankings drop — diagnose before you act

Algorithm updates are the most common reason for unexplained ranking movement. Before making any site changes after a traffic drop, match the timing of your drop to the update log below. If the drop correlates with a known update, the fix is always the same: address whatever that update targeted, not a random collection of "SEO improvements."

Step 01

Identify the date

Note when your traffic dropped in GA4. Cross-reference with GSC's performance data — look at impressions, not just clicks.

Step 02

Match to an update

Find any update that was confirmed within 2 weeks of your drop. Multiple simultaneous updates are common — check all of them.

Step 03

Diagnose the target

Each update targets something specific. Read what it targets and audit your site for exactly those signals — not general SEO issues.

"Chasing an algorithm update without correctly identifying which update hit you is one of the most common — and most expensive — mistakes in SEO."

The Log — Newest First

Algorithm Update History

2025

March 2025 Core Update

March 2025 Core Update

What changed: A broad core update affecting rankings across many niches. As with all core updates, no single factor was targeted — Google improved its overall assessment of page quality, relevance, and E-E-A-T signals.

Who was affected: Sites across all industries, with particular volatility reported in health, finance, and news verticals.

Recovery approach

Core updates have no specific "fix." Audit overall content quality, E-E-A-T signals, and the degree to which your pages genuinely serve users better than competing pages. If you were hit, the answer is improving quality — not technical changes.

2024

August 2024 Core Update

August 2024 Core Update

What changed: One of the most significant core updates in years. Heavily rewarded small, independent, niche sites that had been previously suppressed by large aggregator sites. Targeted AI-generated content produced at scale without editorial oversight.

Who was affected: Large content farm sites saw significant declines. Small specialist publishers saw substantial gains. Reddit and forums received major boosts.

Recovery approach

Focus on original, experience-based content. If your content is produced at volume without genuine expertise or first-hand experience, that is the issue. Quality over quantity — audit your lowest-performing content and either improve or consolidate it.

March 2024 INP Ranking Signal

INP Becomes a Core Web Vital

What changed: Interaction to Next Paint (INP) replaced First Input Delay (FID) as the responsiveness Core Web Vital. INP measures the full latency of all interactions during a page session, not just the first interaction. The threshold for "good" is under 200ms.

Who was affected: Sites with heavy JavaScript on interaction paths — particularly those using large UI frameworks without optimisation. E-commerce sites with complex product pages.

Fix

Measure INP in PageSpeed Insights (field data). Identify the longest interactions with Chrome DevTools. Common causes: synchronous JavaScript on click handlers, layout thrashing, third-party scripts executing on interaction.

March 2024 Spam Update

March 2024 Spam Update

What changed: Targeted three specific spam tactics: scaled content abuse (large volumes of AI-generated or scraped content), site reputation abuse (hosting third-party content on trusted domains to transfer authority), and expired domain abuse (buying expired domains to redirect or republish content).

Who was affected: Content farm networks, parasite SEO practitioners, and expired domain redirect schemes.

Fix

If hit by this update: remove or significantly improve scaled content. If hosting third-party content, apply noindex or disallow. Do not attempt to exploit domain authority through these mechanisms.

2023

November 2023 Core Update

November 2023 Core Update

What changed: Broad quality update with above-average volatility. Continued the trend of rewarding genuinely expert content and suppressing content produced primarily for search engines.

Who was affected: Broad impact. Particular volatility in YMYL niches.

Recovery approach

Standard core update response: comprehensive content quality audit. Focus on pages that lost the most traffic and assess whether they genuinely satisfy user intent better than the pages that replaced them.

October 2023 System Update

Helpful Content System Integrated into Core

What changed: Google announced the Helpful Content System was integrated into its core ranking system rather than operating as a separate classifier. This means helpful content signals are now applied continuously with every core update rather than on a separate rollout schedule.

Implication

There is no longer a separate "helpful content recovery" cycle to wait for. Improvements to content quality now flow through to rankings faster — tied to the normal crawl and indexing cycle.

2022

August 2022 New System

Helpful Content System Launch

What changed: Google launched the Helpful Content System — a site-wide classifier that downranks sites where a significant proportion of content is created primarily for search engines rather than people. Unlike Panda (which assessed individual pages), this system creates a site-wide signal. Even good content on a site with a lot of unhelpful content can be suppressed.

Who was affected: Content farm sites, sites with large volumes of AI-generated or programmatically scaled content, affiliate sites with thin content.

Recovery approach

Audit your full content library. Remove or substantially improve content that was created primarily to rank rather than to genuinely help users. This is a slow recovery — the classifier re-evaluates the site as a whole and may take several months to update after improvements are made.

Foundational Updates — The Ones That Changed Everything

2019 Language Model

BERT

Applied transformer-based NLP to query understanding. Google could now understand context, prepositions, and the relationship between words in a query rather than treating each word as an independent signal. Made keyword-stuffing obsolete and rewarded naturally written, semantically rich content.

2013 Semantic Search

Hummingbird

Replaced keyword-matching logic with semantic query understanding. Google began understanding the intent behind a search rather than matching the literal words. The foundation for everything that followed — topical authority, semantic SEO, and intent-matching all flow from Hummingbird.

2012 Link Spam

Penguin

Targeted manipulative link building — paid links, link schemes, private blog networks, and keyword-stuffed anchor text. Became real-time in 2016, meaning the link graph is now evaluated continuously. Established that unnatural link profiles are a liability, not just a non-benefit.

2011 Content Quality

Panda

Targeted thin, duplicate, and low-quality content. Introduced site-wide quality assessment — a cluster of bad pages could suppress an entire domain's rankings. Killed content farms, article spinners, and doorway pages. The conceptual ancestor of the 2022 Helpful Content System.
